Lower Palisades Restoration Project
Jersey City · Hudson & Bergen Counties, NJ

A Natural Wonder Hidden in the Weeds

This is a project to help fund and organize habitat restoration on the Lower Palisades. The Lower Palisades is the portion of the NJ Palisades that are South of the George Washington Bridge.

Mission

To fund and organize habitat restoration on the Lower Palisades — a vital wildlife corridor where very little natural area still exists.

The Lower Palisades stretch through Hudson and Bergen Counties, forming a narrow natural corridor amid dense neighborhoods and infrastructure.

Restoring native habitat and controlling invasive plants can preserve shelter and movement routes for species that continue to use this landscape.

Our Path to Restoration

We want to restore the Lower Palisades as a connected urban natural area.

The Palisades were nearly destroyed by quarrying in the late 1800s. A grassroots campaign led by the New Jersey State Federation of Women's Clubs helped create the Palisades Interstate Park Commission in 1900.

Land acquisition and long-term stewardship protected the cliffs in Bergen and Rockland Counties. South of that managed parkland, the Lower Palisades remain divided among municipal and private lots without comparable environmental management.

Existing tools such as Jersey City's Palisades Preservation Overlay District provide a foundation, but protecting views and development patterns is not the same as managing an urban cliff ecosystem.

Current Issues

The Lower Palisades need coordinated ecological management, not one-off maintenance.

01 Uncontrolled spread of invasive plants

Tree of Heaven, Kudzu, Japanese Knotweed, English Ivy, Norway Maple

02 Improper natural-area management practices

Just topping plants when they block visibility of roadways and skylines.

03 Trash

Dumping and encampments in difficult areas to clean.

Our Plan

  1. Start Small

    Begin with City-owned lots on the Palisades cliffs, specifically beneath Riverview Park, and establish a repeatable restoration model.

  2. Get Organized

    Create a master environmental-management plan based on urban habitat-restoration practices, then secure funding for invasive plants and trash removal.

  3. Build on Existing Protections

    Connect current ordinances and planning efforts to ecological goals so protection includes the cliff ecosystem as well as skyline views.
